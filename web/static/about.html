About

Thirty-two seasons of last eights

UCLnights11 is a draft game. You're dealt real squads that reached the quarter-finals of the European Cup between 1994–95 and 2025–26, you take one player from each, and the eleven you end up with plays a tournament against the real sides of history.

The deck

There were 256 quarter-finalists in those 32 seasons. 243 of them are in the deck, with 4,658 players between them. The other thirteen have no squad list that can be read, and the archive names them rather than leaving a quiet gap.

Each squad is the one its club's season article on Wikipedia lists. Nothing is added from memory: if the article doesn't list a player, he isn't in the deck, however sure anyone is that he was there.

The ratings

The ratings are this game's own judgement of each player in that season, written by hand. They are not worked out from statistics, because no open source has the numbers to do that for thirty years of European football. Disagree with one? That's half the fun. Memory mode hides them altogether.

The positions

Every player holds up to four positions for that season, most-played first, and can only stand in a formation's place for one of them. From 2013–14 they are measured: every position he started at least once for that club that season, in Transfermarkt's match line-ups (as published, under CC0, by the transfermarkt-datasets project). Only starts count, because a substitute's position isn't recorded. Before 2013–14 no open source records a position for every match, so the positions are this game's judgement, like the ratings: the roles he played that season, without the one-off cameos a line-up would catch. Tested blind on recent squads, nine in ten of the positions written that way were ones the player really started in.

The matches

Every match is simulated from the two elevens. The engine's goal rates are the real home and away averages of 1,962 Champions League matches from openfootball, and it is checked against them: across 20,000 simulated matches its home wins, draws and away wins land within a point or two of the real ones.

Whose it is

An independent, non-commercial fan game. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by UEFA or any club. Club crests belong to their clubs and appear here as they would on a fan site. Squad lists come from Wikipedia under CC BY-SA; positions from 2013–14 from Transfermarkt's line-ups via transfermarkt-datasets (CC0); results from openfootball under CC0.

There are no ads and no tracking. The privacy page says exactly what is kept.
